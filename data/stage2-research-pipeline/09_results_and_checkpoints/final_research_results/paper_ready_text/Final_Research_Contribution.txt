
FINAL RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION
===========================

The study provides a reproducible semi-supervised learning
framework for customer subscription prediction under controlled
label-scarcity conditions.

The experimental results demonstrate that pseudo-labeling can
substantially improve F1-score when only a small portion of
training labels is available.

A key empirical finding is that negative-only pseudo-labeling
achieves performance very close to conventional pseudo-labeling,
revealing asymmetric pseudo-label reliability within the
subscription prediction task.

The explainability contribution is based on two independent
methods—grouped permutation importance and SHAP—which produced
a strong rank correlation and consistently identified Gender
as the dominant predictor.

The study further extends standard predictive evaluation by
performing a structural Gender-Subscription analysis and a
20-seed Gender-ablation sensitivity experiment. Removing Gender
reduced mean F1 by approximately 0.291
(50.9% relative), while ROC-AUC fell
to approximately 0.491.

These results demonstrate the importance of combining
semi-supervised learning with explainability and robustness
analysis, particularly when predictive performance may be
strongly influenced by structural characteristics of a
synthetic dataset.
